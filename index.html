01 / The big idea

What is Industrie 4.0?

Industrie 4.0 is the idea of making industry smarter by connecting physical machines with digital systems. The term became a major German industrial initiative around 2011.

01 — CONNECT

Machines that communicate

Sensors and connected equipment share information, helping factories understand what is happening in real time.

02 — UNDERSTAND

Data with a purpose

Cloud computing, edge computing and analytics turn large amounts of production data into useful insights.

03 — IMPROVE

People make better decisions

Smart systems can support quality checks, maintenance and planning. Human expertise remains important.

Four industrial revolutions

Industry 1.0

Steam power and mechanisation transformed manual work.

Industry 2.0

Electricity enabled mass production and assembly lines.

Industry 3.0

Computers and electronics brought automation to factories.

Industry 4.0

Connected systems, data and cyber-physical systems link the digital and physical worlds.

02 / Inside a smart factory

Technology at work

A modern factory is more than robots. It is a network of machines, software, people and information.

01 / IIoT

Industrial Internet of Things

Sensors connect machines and share operating data, such as temperature, speed or vibration.

02 / AI + DATA

Predictive maintenance

Analytics can spot warning signs early, helping teams plan maintenance before a breakdown.

03 / DIGITAL TWINS

A virtual factory

A digital twin is a virtual model used to simulate, monitor or improve a physical product or process.

04 / LOGISTICS

Smarter supply chains

Tracking systems and connected warehouses improve visibility of parts, stock and deliveries.

05 / ROBOTICS

Humans and robots

Collaborative robots, or cobots, can assist workers with repetitive or physically demanding tasks.

06 / SECURITY

Protecting connected systems

Cybersecurity and data protection help keep connected production systems safe and reliable.

03 / Germany's approach

Engineering with a long view

Germany is known for engineering, industrial manufacturing and a network of small and medium-sized businesses often called the Mittelstand. Industrie 4.0 aims to build on these strengths while adapting to a more digital economy.

Mensch + Maschine

German: “Human + machine.” The goal is not simply more automation, but useful collaboration between technology and people.

  • ⚙️
    Flexible productionManufacturing lines can adapt to different orders and customised products.
  • 🌱
    Greener manufacturingEnergy efficiency, recycling and careful use of resources can reduce waste.
  • 🎓
    Skills for tomorrowTraining and lifelong learning help workers develop digital and technical skills.
  • 🔐
    Trust and securityConnected factories need standards, secure networks and responsible data use.

07 / Language & history

German: a language with history

German belongs to the West Germanic branch of the Indo-European language family. Its written standard developed over centuries, influenced by regional varieties, literature, printing and education.

Today, German is used across Germany, Austria, Switzerland and other communities. Learning a few words can make a technology project feel connected to the people and place behind it.

Did you know?

Long words, clear building blocks

German often combines smaller words to create a longer compound word. For example, Industrie + Revolution forms Industrielle Revolution — Industrial Revolution.

Pronunciation tip: “w” often sounds like English “v”, while “z” usually sounds like “ts”. German “ü” has no exact English equivalent, so listening and practising helps.
